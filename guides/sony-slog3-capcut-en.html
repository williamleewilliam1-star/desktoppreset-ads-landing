Sony S-Log3 LUTs for CapCut Desktop: A Practical Workflow

A LUT is not a universal video filter. Sony S-Log3 footage and Rec.709 footage encode light and color differently. If you apply the wrong LUT at the wrong step, skin tones, saturation and contrast can break even when the file itself works correctly.

1. Identify your footage

Confirm whether your Sony clip was recorded with S-Log3, S-Log2, an S-Cinetone picture profile, or a standard Rec.709 appearance. A creative LUT meant for Rec.709 should not be assumed to perform the technical S-Log3 conversion.

2. Normalize before creative styling

Where the workflow supports it, apply a technically correct transform from the recorded S-Log3 gamut and transfer characteristic to the intended display/output color space first. Then apply a creative look designed for that normalized image. Do not double-transform footage that has already been converted.

3. Import LUTs into CapCut Desktop

CapCut features vary by platform and version. Open the current desktop color adjustment controls, locate the LUT import option when available, and choose the compatible .cube file. If that feature is unavailable in your version, confirm CapCut's current support before purchasing a pack for that workflow. Test a short clip rather than processing the full timeline immediately.

4. Check skin and highlights

Adjust exposure, white balance, and intensity while watching familiar skin tones and neutral highlights. Avoid judging only the thumbnail. Review the image after export on the intended display because mobile screens and desktop viewers may differ.

5. Keep your grading reversible

Save the original media, duplicate a project version before experimenting, and document whether each LUT is a technical conversion or a creative grade. This makes it easier to compare looks and diagnose problems.

Compatibility note: A pack labeled “Sony S-Log3” does not automatically mean each included LUT is a technical S-Log3-to-Rec.709 transform. Read the seller's product specifications and any instructions for the exact pack. Test on representative footage.
