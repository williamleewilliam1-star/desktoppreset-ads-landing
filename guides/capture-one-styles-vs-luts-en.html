Capture One Styles vs LUTs: What Actually Changes?

“Preset,” “style,” and “LUT” are often treated as interchangeable words in photography shops. They are not. Before buying a film-inspired look for Capture One, it helps to know exactly what kind of file your software can use and what adjustments it may affect.

What is a Capture One Style?

A Capture One Style is a collection of image adjustments that can be applied to a photograph. Depending on the Style, this may include elements such as color balance, curves, contrast, and other supported settings. It works in Capture One's photo-editing workflow and can be adjusted after application.

Styles are commonly distributed as individual .costyle files or bundled formats such as .costylepack. Check the exact supported formats for your version of Capture One before importing.

What is a .CUBE LUT?

A .CUBE LUT is a lookup table that maps one set of color values to another. It is frequently used in video workflows such as DaVinci Resolve, OBS Studio, and compatible editors. A .CUBE LUT alone is not the same as a Capture One Style and should not be assumed to import through the same workflow.

How to install Styles in Capture One

  1. Download the compatible Capture One Style files and unzip the archive if needed.
  2. Open Capture One and find the Styles and Presets tool. Its location may differ by workspace and version.
  3. Use the import command provided for Styles and select the relevant .costyle or supported pack file.
  4. Open a representative RAW photograph, apply the imported Style, and review the result.
  5. Adjust white balance and exposure for the original image rather than assuming the same look will work identically on every photograph.

Why the same Style looks different across photographs

Lighting, sensor response, white balance, subject colors, and exposure all affect the resulting look. A warm style that flatters an outdoor portrait can exaggerate orange indoor lighting. Film-inspired styles are a starting point, not a universal camera calibration.

Practical portrait workflow

First establish accurate exposure and skin tones. Apply a Style and evaluate color shifts at 100% and in a full-screen view. Reduce or revise individual adjustments when needed. Compare both neutral and highly saturated objects. Only then export the finished image.
